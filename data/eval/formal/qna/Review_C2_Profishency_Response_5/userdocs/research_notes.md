# Research Notes - The Hobbit

The Hobbit, or There and Back Again, by J. R. R. Tolkien was first published in 1937. It was written for children and is set in the same world as The Lord of the Rings, which was published later, in 1954-1955.

The main character, Bilbo Baggins, is a hobbit: a small, home-loving person who lives in a comfortable hole at Bag End in the Shire. The wizard Gandalf chooses him to join Thorin Oakenshield and twelve other dwarves as their burglar.

The dwarves want to win back their home and treasure in the Lonely Mountain (Erebor), which the dragon Smaug took from them long ago. On the journey Bilbo meets trolls, goblins, Gollum, giant spiders and wood-elves. Smaug is killed by Bard the Bowman at Lake-town, and the story ends with the Battle of Five Armies.

Common themes of the book are courage, growing up, the value of home and the dangers of greed.
