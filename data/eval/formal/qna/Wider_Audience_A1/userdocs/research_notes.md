# Research Notes - Commuting and Car Use

Many people drive to work because it is flexible and comfortable, and because public transport does not always go where they need it. In many cities, longer commutes are linked to growing car ownership and new housing far from city centres.

Some people buy new or bigger cars partly for status. Others keep old cars for a long time because they are cheaper. Older cars often produce more pollution than new ones.

Common ways for governments to reduce traffic include more frequent buses, bus lanes, cycle paths, park-and-ride car parks and congestion charges. Experts say that people only leave their cars at home if the alternative is fast, reliable and affordable.
